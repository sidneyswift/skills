## Words that become the structure of a scene

**Mechanism:** Typography performs the meaning of the words through load, support and space.

**Build:**

1. Choose a small set of words with physical roles.
2. Convert only those words to glyph outlines; maintain counters and readable proportions.
3. Let a readable phrase become a beam, aperture or platform after its reading hold.

**Tune:** Use one deformable element at a time; contrast tension with a deliberate stationary beat.

**Failure check:** Read silently at delivery size. The sentence order and physical metaphor must both survive without narration.

**Adapted prompt:** Give [key words] structural jobs in the composition. Build the ending from forms introduced earlier, after each line has time to read.

**Evidence:** [@Gdgtify](https://x.com/Gdgtify/status/2103458245213929495). Creator description/prompt plus [selected original/remake states](../updates/2026-09-30-type-comparison.md). Letter displacement and a stable two-line result observed; exact spring, masking, blur method and loop seam remain unverified. The remake changes copy and branding.


**Observed study:** [Structural typography comparison](../structural-type-study.md) records the suspended load, word supports and final platform. It separates those observations from the unverified aperture and loop-seam intentions.

## Per-glyph entrances with phrase-level hierarchy

**Mechanism:** Letter motion can add rhythm without destroying the sentence as a unit.

**Build:**

1. Shape text into grapheme clusters with the actual font before positioning.
2. Animate baseline displacement, opacity and optional blur with a shared phrase cue and short per-glyph offsets.
3. End all glyphs on a stable typographic layout.

**Tune:** Stagger by phrase length and available hold; never assume every language is separable into single code points.

**Failure check:** Check long words, ligatures, combining marks and small output size. No letter is still moving when the next phrase covers it. Check the interval between the final glyph becoming readable and the exit beginning; a long entrance is not automatically a long reading hold. Verify the actual wording as well as the layout.

**Adapted prompt:** Animate [short message] as deliberate letter choreography. Keep the reading order and a clean final typeset frame.

## A letter counter becomes the next scene

**Mechanism:** A recognizable opening gives a motivated transition into a new scale or world.

**Build:**

1. Choose a counter or negative-space shape with a clear interior.
2. Render the next scene behind a mask derived from that opening.
3. Expand the camera until the mask fully covers the viewport, then hand off under occlusion.

**Tune:** Preserve the opening shape until recognition; use the viewport corners to determine full coverage.

**Failure check:** Check all aspect ratios and the frame before the handoff. No edge flashes or unrelated path-point explosions.

**Adapted prompt:** Travel through the negative space of [glyph/object] into [next scene], preserving a clear visual relationship.

## Variable font weight as a physical event

**Mechanism:** Weight change can convey pressure or impact when the baseline and optical center remain controlled.

**Build:**

1. Confirm that the chosen font actually supports a weight axis.
2. Drive weight from the action envelope while reserving sufficient layout width.
3. Add a small baseline or scale response only if it reinforces the same action.
4. Offset one shared weight envelope across glyph order; keep tracking and whole-word expansion independently authored.
5. Choose a fixed or deliberately expanding footprint and a stable anchor. See [traveling weight and layout checks](../build-patterns.md#traveling-font-weight).

**Tune:** Use a limited axis range first; compensate optical drift from measured glyph bounds rather than arbitrary offsets.

**Failure check:** Inspect thin, middle and heavy states plus maximum mixed weight. Check tight pairs, clipping, unintended reflow and diagnostic bounds against the actual drawn geometry.

**Adapted prompt:** Make [word] change weight as [force] acts on it. Keep the layout stable and the response causally tied to the event.

**Evidence:** [@Dannnnnok](https://x.com/Dannnnnok/status/2103846630088716687). Original Opus 5.5 attribution/date verified; selected original/remake type phases inspected. [Observation limits](../updates/2026-09-29-weight-wave.md): actual font axis, renderer, sound and frame rate remain unverified.

## Captions built from speech timing

**Mechanism:** The reading unit follows the utterance and emphasis, not a uniform word timer.

**Build:**

1. Align the final voice track to word timestamps.
2. Group words into meaningful short phrases and choose line breaks at delivery size.
3. Highlight or reveal from those timestamps; schedule graphics in the space left by the captions.

**Tune:** Reading hold and safe area take priority over a beat grid. Keep one primary text unit active.

**Failure check:** Listen and read at once. Check fast clauses, pauses, names and the final word after trimming the audio.

**Adapted prompt:** Use the final narration timing to stage [message]. Keep captions readable and let the visual explain what the voice cannot show alone.

## An image formed from meaningful characters

**Mechanism:** Text density and local luminance form an image while the character set carries a second meaning.

**Build:**

1. Rasterize the target into a sampling grid.
2. Map cell luminance to a calibrated glyph-density table from the actual font.
3. Fill cells using a seeded stream of relevant code or symbols, keeping the image silhouette stable.

**Tune:** Tune cell aspect ratio and contrast before adding motion; preserve semantic excerpts only where readable.

**Failure check:** View at thumbnail and full size. The image should read at distance and the characters should remain intentional up close.

**Adapted prompt:** Render [subject] from [meaningful character set]. Preserve the silhouette, then reveal the hidden textual layer through motion or proximity.

## Let the visual system evolve with the story

**Mechanism:** Changes in palette, rendering resolution or ornament become part of the historical explanation.

**Build:**

1. Define period-specific visual rules tied to verified milestones.
2. Maintain one recurring motif while changing its representation across chapters.
3. Coordinate sound and typography changes with the same chapter boundary.

**Tune:** Use a few clearly distinguishable stages; do not claim historical accuracy from a stylistic progression alone.

**Failure check:** Check dates and labels against sources. Without captions, the transitions should still communicate meaningful progression.

**Adapted prompt:** Tell [history] through an evolving visual grammar, with one stable motif connecting the eras and sourced labels for factual claims.

## Explain a design decision through construction

**Mechanism:** Construction lines reveal relationships that a polished final screen hides.

**Build:**

1. Keep one stable coordinate system for the original design.
2. Isolate the relevant module and draw alignment, spacing or grouping relationships.
3. Animate only the actual changed geometry, then return to the finished visual treatment.

**Tune:** One decision per segment. Put explanatory text outside moving regions; avoid decorative dimensions with no meaning.

**Failure check:** Compare the final state to its source. Unchanged modules must not drift; labels must identify a real design consequence.

**Adapted prompt:** Explain why [layout decision] works by exposing its spatial relationships and showing the exact change.
