# Design Inspiration

## Overview

Staring at a blank canvas kills momentum. The fix isn't more creativity —
it's more inputs. Great designers don't invent from nothing; they collect,
remix, and recombine. This skill turns "I'm stuck" into a structured
sweep across nine curated sources, each tuned to a different design
surface, so you always come back with something to react to.

The sources are handpicked for signal density. No Pinterest rabbit holes,
no Dribbble fantasy UI. These are real shipped work, real brand systems,
real design decisions made by people who had to ship.

## When to use

- "I'm stuck on the design"
- "I don't know what this should look like"
- "I need visual inspiration"
- "Show me how other people handle this"
- "What's the design direction here?"
- "This looks boring / generic / off"
- "I need references before I start designing"
- Any variant of "I'm out of ideas visually"

## Process

### Phase 1: Name the surface

Before browsing, identify what you're actually designing. The surface
determines where to look first.

Ask: "What are you designing?" If the answer is vague, narrow it:

- Is it a screen / page / component? → Web & product
- Is it a post / graphic / thumbnail? → Social & content
- Is it a logo / brand / identity system? → Brand & identity
- Is it a deck / OG image / specific artifact? → Specialized

### Phase 2: Sweep the primary source

Go to the source that matches the surface. Spend 5–10 minutes collecting
screenshots or links of anything that resonates — even partially. You're
not looking for "the answer." You're looking for *reactions*: "I like this
spacing," "that color feels right," "this hierarchy works."

Aim for 3–5 references minimum.

### Phase 3: Cross-pollinate

Pick one adjacent source and browse it too. The best design ideas come
from applying patterns from one surface to another:

- Stuck on a landing page? Check how deck designers handle the same
  narrative arc.
- Social post feeling flat? See how OG images solve the same
  constraint (one glance, one message).
- Brand system feels generic? Look at minimal websites for restraint
  cues.

### Phase 4: Extract patterns

From your collected references, pull out the *structural* decisions —
not the surface aesthetics:

- **Layout:** How is information prioritized? What's the visual
  hierarchy?
- **Typography:** How many type sizes? What does the contrast between
  heading and body feel like?
- **Color:** How many colors are actually used? What's doing the heavy
  lifting — color or spacing?
- **Density:** How much breathing room? What's the ratio of content to
  whitespace?
- **Motion / rhythm:** Is there repetition? Asymmetry? Grid breaks?

### Phase 5: Apply to your work

Take the 2–3 strongest patterns and apply them to your actual design.
Don't copy layouts — steal structure. The goal is to restart momentum
with a direction that has evidence behind it, not to replicate someone
else's work.

## Anti-patterns

- **Browsing without a surface in mind.** Inspiration without intent is
  procrastination. Name what you're designing first.
- **Copying aesthetics instead of structure.** Gradients and shadows are
  surface. Hierarchy and spacing are structure. Steal the latter.
- **Collecting 50 references.** You need 3–5 that provoke a reaction, not
  a mood board the size of a museum. More inputs ≠ more clarity.
- **Skipping cross-pollination.** Staying in one source gives you
  category-typical ideas. The interesting moves come from adjacent
  surfaces.
- **Using this instead of designing.** Inspiration is a warmup, not the
  workout. Set a time limit and get back to work.
